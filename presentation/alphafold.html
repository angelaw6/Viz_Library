Google DeepMind · Nature, 2021

AlphaFold

Teaching an AI to see the shape of life — and cracking a 50-year grand challenge in biology.

Jumper et al. “Highly accurate protein structure prediction with AlphaFold.” Nature 596, 583–589 (2021).
Why it matters

Proteins are the
machines of life.

Every muscle, antibody, and enzyme is a protein — and what it does depends on the 3-D shape it folds into.

Know the shape, and you can understand a disease, or design the drug that fits it.

The conflict

We could read the sequence.
We couldn't see the shape.

200M+
protein sequences known from DNA
~0.1%
had a structure solved in the lab

The only way to see a structure — X-ray crystallography, cryo-EM — takes months to years and can cost a PhD's worth of work for a single protein.

The red sliver is every structure humanity had solved. Everything grey was still dark.

Why it was unsolved

Too many ways
to fold.

A single protein could fold in more ways than there are atoms in the universe.

Yet nature does it in a thousandth of a second. Brute force was never going to work — you had to learn the rules.

10300
possible shapes for one modest protein (Levinthal's paradox)
50 years unsolved since Anfinsen, 1972
The breakthrough

AlphaFold reads evolution,
then predicts the fold.

A deep-learning system, trained end-to-end: it lines up related proteins across species, reasons over every pair of amino acids with attention, and builds the 3-D structure directly.

The result · CASP14, 2020

Accurate to the
width of an atom.

On the field's blind test, AlphaFold matched experiments so closely the organizers declared the problem solved.

92.4
median accuracy score / 100 (GDT)
~1 Å
typical error — one atom's width
The impact

From 200,000 structures
to 200 million — free to everyone.

DeepMind released a structure for nearly every known protein. Overnight, biology got its map.

Malaria vaccines Antibiotic resistance Plastic-eating enzymes Faster drug discovery

2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. A grand challenge, closed — and handed to the world.
